HOW 128-BIT TRANSPONDERS STOP RELAY THEFT

An engineering look at rolling cryptographic tokens, high-security immobilizer handshakes, and simple everyday habits to protect your vehicle from radio signal theft.

1. What Is a "Relay Attack" on Keyless Cars?

In a relay attack, two thieves operate in tandem using high-gain radio frequency transceivers:

  • Thief 1 stands outside your front door or window with an amplifier antenna, capturing the faint standby ping from your smart key resting on the hall table.
  • Thief 2 stands next to your car in the driveway holding a companion receiver.

The amplifier bridges the 40-foot distance in milliseconds, fooling the vehicle into thinking the owner is standing right next to the driver door with the key. The car unlocks, push-to-start activates, and the thieves drive away in under 60 seconds without ever touching your physical keys.

⚠

Are You Leaving Your Fob Near the Front Entryway?

Over 78% of relay thefts occur between 1:00 AM and 4:00 AM because car owners hang their key rings on wall hooks or drop them in decorative bowls within 15 feet of front doors or exterior walls.

2. How 128-Bit AES Rolling Code Cryptography Works

To counter replay attacks, tier-1 automotive suppliers (Bosch, Denso, Continental) implemented 128-bit Advanced Encryption Standard (AES) rolling transponders.

Older fixed-code chips broadcasted the exact same radio ID string every time. Today, the transponder and car ECU share a secret 128-bit cryptographic key. When the car transmits a random challenge number (e.g. 9F82A4...), the fob calculates an encrypted response hash. Once verified, both modules step their internal counter forward. If a radio interceptor records that transmission, it is already invalid by the time they attempt to replay it.

3. The Newest Weapon: Ultra-Wideband (UWB) Time-of-Flight

Beginning with 2022+ BMW, Audi, Genesis, and Ford models, manufacturers introduced Ultra-Wideband (UWB) chips. UWB does not just measure radio signal strength (which amplifiers can fake); it measures the speed-of-light time-of-flight down to picoseconds.

Because radio waves cannot travel faster than the speed of light, the milliseconds added by a relay antenna reveal to the vehicle computer that the key is actually 40 feet away inside the house, instantly aborting ignition permission.

4. Why You Must Erase Lost Keys When Fobs Are Stolen

If your purse or backpack was stolen with your car keys inside, having a new key cut is only half the job.

A certified automotive locksmith plugs into your car's OBD-II port to execute an All-Keys-Wipe Protocol. This deletes every registered transponder ID stored in the Body Control Module and re-registers only the keys currently in your possession. If the thief returns with your old fob, the immobilizer blocks fuel delivery and the engine will not turn over.

5. 4 Simple Habits to Protect Your Vehicle at Home

1

Use a Faraday RF Blocking Pouch or Box

A dual-layer metallic mesh pouch blocks 100% of RF transmission, rendering amplifier tools completely blind.

2

Disable Key Fob Sleep Mode (On Supported Makes)

Many Mercedes, Toyota, and BMW smart keys allow you to put the fob to sleep by double-pressing lock while holding unlock.

3

Store Keys at Least 25 Feet from Exterior Walls

Moving keys to a kitchen drawer or interior bedroom dramatically reduces amplifier pickup sensitivity.

4

Program a Spare Key Before You Lose Your Last One

Adding a spare key takes 15 minutes and costs half as much as an emergency all-keys-lost recovery.
